> "now that the repair lands way more … an LLM can scrutinize single rows based on the rules we

Over the 318 sheets since the repair fix (`4144c00`):

```

  dropped below the confidence gate ........... 164   30%
  on a print/engine row — rewrite blocked ......  34    6%
  routed to the free-row repair ................  74   14%   -> repaired 72, declined 31 (of 103 routings incl. 11-10)
  the rest: renames (57), applied rewrites (10), or silently unrouted

repaired free rows on a FAILing row ........... 3 of 59 (5.1%)  vs 17 of 894 (1.9%) for every other row
re-audit after a repair ....................... never (6 of 6 repaired sheets: one audit pass)
```

**Why the sound blocks did not arrive — four causes, in order of size.**

1. **The gate throws away a third of what the LLM found, and most of it is real.** 164 of 540. By the
   row they target: 45 chassis, 34 print, 31 FREE (repairable!), 19 template. Read them: *"Reel states a
   save and range but no action timing"*, *"the Restrained rider applies 'while Grappled' without
   stating it is contingent on this Bite's grapple"*, *"Swallow stacks three distinct end-conditions"*.
   Those are soundness. The gate is `confidence ∈ {high, medium}` (principle 5 high only; an ABSENT
   confidence is coerced to `low` and dropped — `audit.mjs:334-340`). This is Item R, and it is the
   biggest lever in the engine right now.
2. **The fix can only touch one kind of row.** Chassis, print and template rows are "rewrite blocked";
   only a FREE row (≈1 per sheet) reaches the repair. So the audit scrutinizes 6–8 rows and can act on
   one. The 45 chassis-row and 19 template-row findings above have NO fix path at all — a template
   row's fix is a re-fit (pick another template) or a re-render, not a free-text rewrite, and that
   path does not exist.
3. **Nothing re-audits.** The repair's output is checked for emptiness, raw tokens and dangling
   refs — nothing else (H6). Result: repaired rows FAIL at 2.7× the rate of other rows, each on a
   DIFFERENT gate than the one that sent them (`name-mechanic`, `save-fit`, `grapple-no-escape`).
   The LLM's scrutiny is spent once, on the row that is then replaced unscrutinized.
4. **The checker cannot see most of what the audit fixes.** 51% of findings are the long tail — dead
   clauses, ambiguous timing, a referent that is not a row, a name that over-promises — and the
   checker's gates are hard rules (escape DC, dice, vocabulary). So "more sound blocks" partly
   happened and was not measured: the FAIL rate is the wrong instrument for the audit's kind of
   soundness. The instrument that sees it is the owner's hand read on `review.html`.

**The opportunity is one design, not four fixes: ITEM R, reframed.**
- (a) The gate becomes a POINT SYSTEM (owner's ask, 11-10): principle weight × confidence × whether
  the row is reachable; a `medium` on a free row with a verbatim span is spent, a `low` with no span
  is logged. Owner sets the weights.
- (b) A finding on a TEMPLATE row routes to a re-fit, not a rewrite: the fit call is shown the finding
  and the menu minus that template. A finding on a CHASSIS row routes to the deterministic fixers that
  already exist (`repairMultiattackAttackList`, caps, the lint's correct/reject loop) or is surfaced
  as a review note — never dropped silently.
- (c) The repaired row passes the engine's own refusals before it lands (H6/H8), and the audit's own
  complaint is re-checked against the new text (the verbatim span must be GONE).
- (d) Surface the dropped findings on `review.html` as "the audit saw, the engine did not act" — so
  the owner's read stops rediscovering what the LLM already said.

Measure before building (a): re-score the 164 dropped findings by hand on ONE twelve — how many would
the owner have acted on? That number sets the weights.

## ITEM R2 — room for BLOCK-LEVEL fixes (owner, 2026-11-11 night: "there are holistic errors that need catching too")

**Measured.** The audit already reads the WHOLE block (`renderStatblock2024(monster)` in its prompt)
and asks for cross-row defects (principle 3, self-consistency). It finds them — **37 of 553
findings** — and **33 of the 37 were dropped by the confidence gate**, never reaching a fix. The fix
that exists is per-row ("THE ROW TO REPAIR — it is called X… KEEP THE NAME"), so a finding about the
RELATIONSHIP between rows is handed to a call that can only rewrite one of them, usually the wrong
one. Holistic errors are found and discarded almost without exception.

**Is there room? Three answers, all yes.**

1. **In the call budget — yes, at zero net cost.** The stage spends 0.85 audit + 0.29 per-row fix +
   0.10 clause-repair calls per sheet. A block-level fix is ONE call carrying the whole block and
   every finding (row-level and cross-row), returning edits per row; it REPLACES the up-to-two per-row
   calls rather than adding to them. Fewer calls on a sheet with two findings, the same on a sheet
   with one.
2. **In the fixers — mostly already written, no call needed.** The 37 cross-row findings by what
   would fix them:
   - 16 referent drift ("the cube" / "Cube" / "Larder" / "vess's") → the checker has an
     `inline-consistency` SOFT and the engine has no pass; one deterministic pass over row text
     substituting the sheet's declared `inline` is the fix, and it needs no model.
   - ~6 "no entry in the block applies Grappled/Frightened" → the ship-time enabler (`payoff enabled
     at ship`, `generate.mjs:~1394`) already welds the missing rider when a row DECLARES
     `requiresCondition`; H13's Tentacle case showed the requirement living in prose instead. Route
     the audit's finding to it: the finding IS the declaration.
   - 4 "two rows duplicate / contradict" (Riposte beside Answering Strike, Scurry beside a Disadvantage
     trait) → the family cap (H10b) catches the template pairs; an authored duplicate is the one case
     that wants a call — the block-level one.
   - 3 "a term the block never defines" (a 'terror' with no row) → `unplaced`/description, exists.
   - 1 Multiattack names a row that is not there → `repairMultiattackAttackList`, exported, exists.
   - a few edition/format → the lint's surgery, now re-run after repairs.
   So **~30 of 37 route to code that exists**, and the remainder is exactly what a block-level
   call is for.
3. **In the architecture — yes, and the slot is where the per-row repair sits today** (~10435,
   after the audit, before the final lint/derivations), with the block fully assembled. Nothing new
   has to be built around it.

**Shape (for Item R, not to build before the owner's read):** findings are ROUTED BY WHAT THEY ARE
