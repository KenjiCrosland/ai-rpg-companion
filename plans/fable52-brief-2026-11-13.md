## Bottom line

The pipeline is good at producing rules-legal rows and cannot tell whether the GM got the creature
they asked for. Read from statblock-ability-enhancements at abf3cdb; I ran no tests and generated no
blocks.

Three design facts cause most of the rejects, and none is a missing patch:

1. A donor is a whole creature, not a math budget. Routing to one of 93 donors decides the ability
   scores, skills and speed before the concept is consulted.
2. A written row can only say damage, one of ten conditions, or a push or pull. Asks outside that
   set get the ask's name on a generic row.
3. The checker scores stamps, not outcomes. It gave full coverage to 9 of my 11 rejects.

Recommended order: fix the instrument, narrow the role filter, move the body upstream into a
creature spec, then widen what a row can say. This is not a rewrite. The route, donors, templates,
value check and lint all stay.

## Order of work

Reconciled with the planning persona's reply of 11-13. Items on different code run in parallel.

1. **Route: split role from family for artillery and controller only.** Brute, skirmisher, lurker
   and defender keep today's filter. For artillery and controller the shelf adds the non-magic lines
   that do the job (hurler and archer; grappler and hurler), and the no-magic veto at route.mjs:510
   applies with a role picked. The donor and the ability menu still come from the one routed line.
   Done when: Big Bob routes off the caster family on 5 of 5 rolls, the two caster controls stay
   casters, and every templated row on the v-route blocks lists the routed family in its roles.
2. **The small items, in parallel.** Rider on the attack, always. The monk's kitRider survives the
   print rename (3 of 3 monks; moves two of my reworks). They/them for a named Humanoid. Template
   names stop leaking. A compound with a print head ("vampire pirate") routes as a twist on print.
   Done when: each known-bad sheet replays clean.
3. **The instrument.** For each input, one offline call writes three to six things a GM would
   expect, typed and implied. Freeze the list per batch. A judge call marks each item met, partial
   or missing from the row's mechanics, never its stamp. It adds no call to a generation, and it
   informs your read; it does not replace it. Done when: on today's forty it flags at least 9 of the
   11 rejects and none of the 5 best.
4. **Ability profile on the existing identity call.** That call already returns mind, size,
   movement and creature_type. Add ability_rank and a finer mind tier. The array derives from the
   profile; the donor keeps AC, HP, attack bonus, DC, damage budget and row shapes. No new call. Done
   when: the same input under two different chassis lines returns the same rank, two sheets share an
   array only if the GM named the same print body, and the vampire, mind flayer and wolf read right.
5. **Widen the effect vocabulary by miss frequency**, in the order the [gap] and signature miss:
   logs ask. Candidates from this forty: speed reduction, legendary actions composed from the
   sheet's own rows, an ally-adjacent bonus, on-hit retaliation, healing denial, a form reveal, and
   summon or raise. Numbers stay the engine's. Done when: the judge finds no row whose name promises
   an effect its text lacks.
6. **An honest exit at the seat.** An ask with no matching effect is visibly owed, not shipped as a
   named damage row.
7. **Repair forms for a trait and an attack row.** The repair declined 9 times on this forty, 6 for
   want of a form.

### Decisions, ruled by the owner on 11-13.

- **Implied identity: loosened.** This narrows "no published trait lands on a word alone"
  (generate.mjs:8785). An implied item becomes an ask through the engine's own effects and numbers.
  **At most two per sheet. Never a pasted print row.**
- **Summon or raise: back, with guardrails.** Only when the GM asked for it; never implied, never
  dealt blind. Gated 1/Day or by recharge. It names one existing SRD block, with a count, a duration
  and when the summoned creature acts. The engine sets the summoned creature's CR ceiling from the
  sheet's CR, and the row is priced against the damage budget.
- **Honest exit: tell the GM.** One line on the card naming what was not statted.
- **Release bar: decided together.** The test is whether a block feels *made*, not *generated*. See
  the release section below.

Every production call stays on deepseek-flash. The model is held fixed until the architecture items
are green, so a model change cannot be mistaken for a fix.

On tests: items 2 and 3 warrant routing and data-integrity tests (role does not narrow the shelf; a
spec profile yields a coherent array). I would agree those categories with you before writing any.
The judge is checked against hand reads, not unit tests.

## Validation points

Every item climbs the same ladder, and a rung is not started until the one below is green.

1. **The call alone.** Fixed inputs, several rolls, no generation around it. Check that the answer
   parses and that its fields are right. The repo already does this for two calls
   (smoke-replay-resolve.mjs, probe-repair-chain.mjs).
2. **The deterministic step alone.** Whatever turns the call's fields into numbers or text is a
   pure function. This is where Jest belongs.
3. **A small set of whole blocks.** Eight to twelve, to test the wiring only.
4. **The forty.** Release only.

## What each call must return

A call is accepted on its own answer, before anything downstream runs. Three rules hold for every
call:

- A missing field is a rejection and a retry, never a default. Today validateRouteResponse passes
  any answer with one usable pick, and the engine fills in the rest.
- Every claim about the GM's words carries a quote, and the quote is found in the input.
- Every reference to a row is an exact row name on the sheet. A near miss is a rejection, not a
  trace line.

| Call | Returns | Accept when | Reject when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expectation list (new) | expectations[]: id, text, kind (identity, ask, number), source (typed, implied), quote | Three to six items. Each typed item's quote is in the input. Each implied item names a noun that is in the input. Every stated number (AC 17, CR 8) is an item. | An item about balance or formatting. Two items that say the same thing. |
| Judge (new) | verdicts[]: id, verdict (met, partial, missing), row, why | One verdict per expectation id and no extras. row is an exact row or header field. why quotes that row's text. | "Met" with no row. A row that is not on the sheet. |
| Route (existing) | picks[] with line and quote, casts, hold_shape, creature_type, size | A pick on the full shelf with its quote in the GM's words. Every enum field present and in its enum. | casts.yes is false and the pick is a caster line. That is the Big Bob answer. |
| Ability profile (on the identity call) | type, size, speeds, senses, languages, mind (enum), ability_rank | ability_rank lists all six scores once each. Every non-walking speed has a quote or an implied noun. INT is not ranked last when mind is average or higher. | A score missing or repeated. A speed with no source. A rank that changes when the chassis line changes. |
| Ask to effect (extends the clause call) | clauses[]: id, text, kind, effect (enum, including none), polarity | text is a verbatim piece of the input. effect is in the enum. "None" is stated, not left blank. | A clause the GM did not type. An effect outside the enum. |
| Fit and author (existing) | Template or effect id, name, the effect's fields, answered[] | The id is on the offered menu or is none. Every required field of that effect is filled from its enum. Each answered.row matches a row on the sheet exactly. | A placeholder such as .... A template's default name. An answered.row that matches nothing: retry, do not carry on. |

Report four numbers per call from each probe: first-try parse rate, valid-field rate, agreement with
the hand key, and agreement between rolls. The bar is 95% first-try parse and 100% after one retry.

Each validator is a pure function over the returned JSON. That makes it a fair Jest target: feed it
saved real answers, good and bad, and assert accept or reject. No test reads a prompt string.

## Release green light (settled 11-13 between the owner and Fable 5.2)

- **Run two forties:** typed40, and a fresh one Fable 5.2 writes at gate time so nothing can be
  tuned to it. Each reader reads both without the other's marks, then compare.
- **No screenshot defects.** A screenshot defect is anything a skeptic could post with no caption:
  broken or cut-off text, a leaked template name, a creature that contradicts its own noun, a row
  whose name promises what its text lacks. Target: zero broken text (lint can guarantee it) and at
  most one of the other kinds per forty.
- **No sameness.** Run one prompt five times, and five different prompts in one role. No two sheets
  share an ability array or a signature row. Across a forty, no effect phrase appears on more than
  5 sheets.
- **A spark.** At least half the blocks have one row either reader would steal for their own table.
- **The random five.** Draw five blocks at random, three times. Ship when both readers would post
  all fifteen unedited.
- **Not worse on the old rulers.** Checker FAILs at 4 or fewer; rejects at 3 or fewer, from 11.
