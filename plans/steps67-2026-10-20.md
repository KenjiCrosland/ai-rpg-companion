# Five Pipelines, steps 6 and 7 — the retirements and the homebrew pipeline

**Trigger:** work on the reskin engine's routing, the chassis-line decision, or any of the
rules in the brief's Retirements table. Read this before touching `route.mjs chooseLine`,
`printCanonFor`, or the hint-verdict path.

Steps 1–5 of the "Path Classification and the Five Pipelines" brief (owner, 2026-10-18) are
landed and verified. This file covers what remains.

## Step 7 — the homebrew pipeline

The brief's retirement reads: *"the concept's VERB outranks the noun" as a route decision →
dispatch on class and print_body; style keys move into the resolve call on the homebrew path.*

**What the code already had.** The route call ranks up to three lines, quotes the GM's words
for each, and the engine verifies every quote (`quotedPick`). Since 2026-10-16 it also answers
`cue_verdicts` — whether each key word the engine found really means that chassis's mechanic
here — so the override was already gated on the call's own judgment. The keys were still the
**decision**: a confirmed key promoted its line over the call's own top pick.

**What landed (2026-10-20).** On the homebrew path, when the call's chosen pick carried a
verified quote, the override stands down and says so in the trace. The keys remain as (a) the
menu the call is shown and (b) the fallback when no pick verifies or the family fell to its
plain line. Every other path is untouched, and the blank roll is untouched because the
retirement rests on the call quoting *the GM's own words* and a blank roll has none.

The route prompt now also demands one consistent answer: **a cue you confirm is a pick you
make.** Confirming a key and then ranking a different chassis is one answer contradicting
itself. This is the mechanism that is supposed to preserve the override's good outcomes
without the engine overruling the call.

`chooseLine` takes `path`; the component now makes the *choice* after the path call resolves
(the two calls still race, so there is no added latency) and the *parse* in `routeOn`.

**Why this needed a probe, not just pins.** This flip was attempted and reverted on 2026-10-16
because it broke six pins. Those pins mock the model's answer, so they cannot show whether the
call now ranks correctly — they only show what the engine does with a fixed answer. Three of
them (the giant crab → grappler, the miller who turns into a boar → shifter, the drowned wife →
phantom) are cases where the mocked pick is the *wrong* line and the key corrects it. They pass
unchanged because they pass no path, but that means the suite no longer covers live homebrew.
`scripts/probe-line.mjs` closes that gap: one route call per sample, the same answer fed to
both mechanisms, so the comparison carries no call variance. The three pin cases ride as
labelled traps.

## The homebrew path's dropped Multiattack — found, and it was the chassis

The Squallwing Drake shipped one Claw and no Multiattack at CR 7. My first read of the trace
blamed the GM's named parts replacing the chassis attack rows and the Multiattack leaving with
them. **That was wrong.** `landAuthored` rewrites a Multiattack it finds (`if (ma && …)`) and
never creates one, and the real cause is upstream: **the `dragon-2` chassis is a CR 2 donor
with no Multiattack at all**, scaled to CR 7. `scaleDonor` scales numbers, not shape, so a
low-CR donor carries its low-CR row set up the ladder.

Ten of the ninety donors carry no Multiattack, and eight of those are CR 1–3 bodies that are
perfectly correct at their own CR. Print settles what happens when they are scaled: a
Multiattack on **110 of 113 blocks at CR 5 and up**, against 91 of 106 single-attack blocks
below CR 1. Modal count: **two at CR 5–10, three at CR 11+** (measured, 108 blocks). One
attack row cited N times is print's own idiom — 70 of its 110 CR 5+ Multiattacks name a single
row ("the dragon makes three Rend attacks") — so a one-row chassis gets exactly that.

It lands in `scaleDonor`, **beside the swarm strip and before the per-hit solve**, so the round
is priced for the attack count the sheet actually makes. Putting it later would double the
round past its card, which is the mirror of the bug the swarm strip's comment records.

The swarm exemption is **structural** (`donor.archetypes` includes `swarm`), not a dependence
on the caller passing `oneAttack`: print's seven swarms each make one attack and the checker
hard-fails a swarm that carries a Multiattack, so a swarm chassis must never gain one even if a
caller forgets the flag. `swarm-9` and `swarm-17` do carry an inherited one in the library,
which `oneAttack` still strips — that is pre-existing and unchanged.

# Package five's consolidated brief — section 1 (2026-10-20)

The critic's consolidated read made section 1 the one blocker for the public post: signature
drops, where the menu is chosen by the monster's archetype rather than the move's shape. Landed
below in the brief's own order. **1e and the back half of 1f are not done** and are described at
the end.

## 1a — the menu is chosen by the shape of the move

`templatesFor` takes `shapes` (a list of catalog `(trigger, effect)` pairs) and appends the
matching templates **across roles**, capped at 6, after the role shelf and the key-asked ones.
Appending rather than replacing means role stops *excluding* without any recall being lost, and
a pick the shelf already had keeps its order (the re-roll's reel pin).

The shape comes from the slot's own tag: the idea's `(trigger, effect)` for a free signature
slot, unioned with the catalog shapes the GM's own clauses asked for. The clause reader's
`asked` vocabulary and the catalog's shape tags are two different enums, so `shapesForAsked` is
the translation between them — **enum to enum, a closed fact, not a reading of words.**

Measured against the critic's three named cases:

| case | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Meshrin's roar → `terror-pulse` (skirmisher-hurler) | cut | **offered** (menu 17 → 23) |
| Krennik's standard → `rally` (skirmisher) | cut | **offered** (menu 9 → 10) |
| Glacier Heart's plough → `trample` (brute-juggernaut) | cut | **offered** |

**The critic's diagnosis was half right on Glacier Heart, and finding out mattered.** `trample`'s
roles are `['skirmisher','brute']`, so the role filter never cut it — the **style** filter did,
because `trample` is tagged `styles: ['mounted']` and the body was a juggernaut. Print's
tramplers are the Gorgon, the Triceratops and the Elephant, none of them mounts. Style is a
taste filter exactly like role, so it now sits beside role and steps aside for a shape match.

## 1b — the asked tag persists

The clause is its own quote. The call is answering *about that clause*, so demanding a second
quote from inside it threw the shape away for nothing. A **foreign** quote still drops, because
pointing at words the GM never wrote is a contradiction; silence does not.

**One pin's outcome changed by design:** "an answer with no quote is dropped" was the rule 1b
reverses. Re-expressed, with the foreign-quote half kept as its own new pin.

## 1c — a refusal names its gap

The fit schema gains `closest` and `missing`, and the prompt says what a usable answer looks
like: the mechanic, not the fiction, never "nothing fits", never a restatement of the idea.
Every decline now traces one line in a fixed shape — `[gap] <mechanic> — <clause> — <sheet>`, or
`[gap] UNNAMED` when the call refused without naming one — so runs can be grepped into the list
of templates worth building.

## 1d — the meat-body verb rule retired

This was the biggest single named cause: it killed **five picks in one 2014 file**, among them
the Hydra's Smouldering Stumps, keyed on "reek" when the GM had typed "smoke". It had become a
keyword gate of the kind stage 4b retires, and it fired on rows the GM asked for.

One receipt gate replaces both declines: a trait stands when the call quoted the GM's own words
(`asked`, vetted upstream) or bound on a concept key, which is the GM's word by definition. The
sniper's tracker — the case the rule was written for — still fails the same gate, on any body.

**Two pins' outcomes changed by design**, both encoding the material-body exemption that went
with the rule: a receiptless shape fit no longer ships on a Construct either. The ceiling test
now uses a concept that earns its trait a receipt, so it still tests the ceiling.

## 1f — half of it was already fixed, and the critic read a stale run

"X instead of Y is two clauses" is **already implemented** by a different mechanism (the
positive-half reader), and three pins cover it — including one named for this exact case. I
wrote the split, it broke those three pins, and reverting was correct. Verified live on the
fresh twist run: `Hydra whose stumps smoke instead of regrowing` ships
**"Choking Stump-Smoke (1/Day)"** beside the removed regrowth.

The critic's "smoke dropped" came from the **stale** twist-ten (00:06), which predates the
package-five fixes. Worth noting for the next read: per-path sets must be re-run before they go
in a package.

## 1g — seeds are measured where they live

`generation.signature_rows` and `generation.seed_rows` are stamped per block, traced as "seed
share", and the review table carries `[seed n/m]`. This is the sameness metric the owner sees as
"Opportunist's Lash again", and it is now a column rather than a hunt through traces.

## Not done

- **1e — owed clauses.** The largest item: on twist and class paths every clause that sets the
  request apart from print is owed a row whatever the clause reader called it; the signature
  idea must start from the GM's clause rather than the identity paragraph; repair joins both
  pipelines' legal calls; and repair returning no rows on an owed clause becomes a **FAIL**
  rather than a trace line. This is a structural change to two pipelines plus a new failure
  mode, and it should be its own landing with its own trio.
- **1f, back half.** A LACKS clause with no row to remove should land a **flaw row** (the

# The owner's two rulings (2026-10-20)

## The golem stamp — the word stands down

**Ruled: yes, stand it down.** The name-canon stamp read the WORD "golem" and stapled print's
golem traits (Immutable Form, Magic Resistance) onto a sheet whose path call had answered
`print_body: null` with the receipt "lashed timber". Its only brake was a *second* call
answering `golem → canon ✗`, so the staple landed whenever that call missed.

A path that named no published body now takes no published trait from a word, and the trace
says so. The catalog is where a genuinely golem-like homebrew earns its traits. This retires
the last row of the brief's Retirements table. Pinned both ways: the homebrew golem gets
nothing, and a twist on the Troll still takes print's own Regeneration.

## 4c — an exact name on a 2014 sheet is the 2014 book's creature

**Ruled: serve the real 2014 block, narrow version.** Twists on a 2014 sheet still start from
the 5.2 body; widening that stays a product decision.

The two books hold different monsters, which is the whole point: the 5.1 Vampire is AC 16
(Natural Armor) / 144 HP (17d8+68), the 5.2 one AC 16 / 195. A GM on 2014 rules who typed
"Vampire" was getting the 5.2 creature with 2014 phrasing laid over it.

Cheaper than expected: `print-2014.mjs` already read the 5.1 corpus, and the corpus records are
already in the legacy model's shape, so `exact2014Printing` is a rename and a provenance stamp
rather than a conversion. **Nothing is rephrased and no call is made**, which is what makes it
worth doing — the translation call is exactly what section 4b of the brief wants thinned.

Live: Vampire, Owlbear and Druid all served from 5.1 with `[path] exact`, and the Vampire's card
reads `16 (Natural Armor)` / `144 (17d8 + 68)`. The twist control (`Vampire | who keeps a garden
of thorns`) correctly stays on the 5.2 body.

### A gap the ruling's own smoke found

**The two corpora do not share every name.** 5.1 has "Goblin" where 5.2 has "Goblin Warrior",
and the path call's `print_body` validator checked the 5.2 book alone — so a 2014 GM typing
"Goblin" had a real print name refused and the sheet fell to homebrew.

A name print carries in *either* book is a print name; the **edition** decides which block gets
served, not whether the name is real. `printNameOf` and `corpusEntry` now take the edition and
consult the 2014 corpus only on a 2014 sheet, so a 2024 sheet can never dispatch "Goblin" as
exact and then find no 5.2 block to serve. Pinned on both editions.

## 1f's back half — the flaw row (owner's ruling), landed with one open defect

**Ruled: write one line in print's grammar.** Print carries a costing trait on only 11 of 329
blocks, and every one is keyed to an EXTERNAL trigger (sunlight, bright light, taking Fire
damage) — there is no print flaw for a missing faculty, so this is authored, in print's
sentence form and nowhere beyond it.

Which faculty is the **call's** answer: `takes` is already a validated enum on the lacks
record, so the only thing the engine owns is the enum → sentence table, a closed fact. One flaw
row per sheet, matching print's rarity. The checker's `lack-honored` gate now accepts a flaw row
as honouring the lack, which is the coverage-credit half of 1f.

A lacks clause that removed nothing is detected by fingerprinting the sheet around each clause,
so no removal site had to be touched.

### Two defects the smoke found in my own first version

1. **The name.** I named the row from the GM's words — "Blind in One Eye" — and the
   `name-mechanic` gate rightly failed it in **4 of 5 runs** for promising the Blinded condition
   the text never imposes. Print names a flaw by its CAUSE, never by a condition ("Sunlight
   Sensitivity", "Sunlight Weakness", "Aversion to Fire"), so the name now follows print's
   "<noun> Weakness" pattern off the enum and the GM's words stay on the row as its `because`.
2. **The sentence contradicted the GM.** The call answered `takes: 'part'` for a blind eye, so
   the row read "Disadvantage on attack rolls made with the limb it has lost". The `takes`
   guidance in the lacks prompt now distinguishes a lost SENSE from a lost LIMB.

After both: **5 of 5 land a flaw row, 0 of 5 FAIL** (was 4 of 5 with 4 FAILs).

### CLOSED — and the fix was doctrinal, not another prompt tweak

My own recommendation (a `takes_quote` receipt) would **not** have worked, and saying so matters
more than the fix: validating that a quote exists tells you nothing about whether a blind eye is
a sense or a limb. The receipt contract works for `casts`, `holds` and `bane` because those are
yes/no questions whose evidence IS the quote.

The real fault was that the engine was doing the meaning step. The call was asked `takes` —
*what is removed* — and the engine then **inferred** which roll the wound spoils. Those are two
different questions, and the second is a judgement, which CLAUDE.md's first ruling puts on the
call.

So the lacks answer gained `costs`: which roll the wound spoils, as a closed enum
(`sight | hearing | one_limb_attacks | all_attacks | none`), validated like every other enum,
with the engine rendering print's sentence around whichever the call names. The flaw row keys on
`costs`, never on `takes`.

**5 of 5 now name the sight the GM wrote, 0 of 5 FAIL** — the same ~100% the other call fields
reach once the question put to the call is the one that actually needs judging.

### The record of what it was (kept, because the measurement is the argument)

Before: `blind in one eye` came back `takes: 'part'` in 3 of 5 runs even with tightened
guidance, so the row read "Disadvantage on attack rolls that use the limb it has lost" — a true
sentence about the wrong wound. Two blind alleys rejected on the way, both worth not repeating:
a vaguer sentence (an unconditional blanket Disadvantage exceeds print's conditional version,
and text engineered to be unfalsifiable is worse than text that is sometimes wrong), and
validating `takes` against the quote with a word rule (the first ruling forbids it).

### OPEN: nothing on 1f — superseded by the above

`blind in one eye` comes back as `takes: 'part'` in 3 of 5 runs even with the tightened
guidance, so the row reads "Disadvantage on attack rolls that use the limb it has lost" — a
true sentence about the wrong wound. The gate passes and the block ships, so this is a FICTION
defect, not a rules one, and it is visible only by reading the row.

Deliberately **not** patched by making the `part` sentence vaguer: an unconditional blanket
"Disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks" is far stronger than print's conditional
version, and a sentence engineered to be unfalsifiable is worse than one that is sometimes
wrong about the fiction.

Nor by validating `takes` against the quote with a word rule — that is what CLAUDE.md's first
ruling forbids, and it is the shape the whole sprint has been retiring.

**Recommended next:** one more iteration on the lacks prompt, measured the same way (five runs,
read at the row), and if the call still will not separate a sense from a limb, give the lacks
answer its own `takes_quote` so the engine can check which words the call was reading — the
same receipt contract `casts`, `holds` and `bane` already use, each of which answers correctly
on ~100% of blocks.

# A2 — the save stat (critic's section 2), landed; print overruled C3

**The defect as reported:** two passes disagreed, and the later one won because it ran last.
C3's pass ("the save follows the effect") retyped a mental save on a damage row; the older pass
("the save follows the condition") then read the condition back out of the row's text.

**The real fault in the older pass:** it read the condition the row *requires* as the condition
it *causes*. Glacier's Rebuke, the Downed Rider's Whip and the Bog Maw's Lash all rolled
Strength for rows that impose nothing of the kind — they only apply to a target already prone or
grappled. Print's grammar separates the two cleanly: of its 221 "has the X condition" phrases,
**220 are impositions and the single exception sits inside an if-clause**. So precondition
clauses (`if the…`, `with Advantage if…`, `while…`, `against…`, `unless…`) are excised before
the read. Pinned with six discrimination cases plus the corpus measurement.

**And print overruled the critic on the precedence.** C3 reads "a condition the row hands out
ALONGSIDE its damage never picks the stat". Print says the opposite, without exception:

| printed row (deals damage AND imposes a condition) | save |
|---|---|
| Frightened | **Wisdom 3 of 3** |
| Prone | **Strength or Dexterity 13 of 13** |
| Poisoned | **Constitution 5 of 5** |

Across all 31 such rows the imposed condition picks the stat every time. So the *older* pass's
rule is print's rule, and C3's pass was wrong to override it. C3's pass now yields whenever the
row really imposes a mental condition, and keeps only the case it was built for: a mental save
on a damage row with **no imposed mental condition behind it** — fear in the flavour, not in the
rules. The two passes no longer disagree, with print as the arbiter rather than run order.

### Two wrong turns on the way, both caught by the live smoke

1. **I skipped damage rows** in the later pass, reasoning that C3's pass owned them. The control
   regressed: Breach Surge deals damage *and* imposes Prone, and print pairs Prone with Strength
   or Dexterity whether or not dice are involved. Damage rows belong in the pass.
2. **Then the fire cone flipped to Wisdom** and I read it as a defect, because the critic had
   named it as one. Measuring print first showed Wisdom is correct there — which is what sent me
   to the precedence question instead of patching the symptom.

Neither showed up in the gate. Both needed the four-block smoke read at the row.

# A1 — not done, and the brief points at the wrong layer

A1 as written ("price the round with the payoff firing against the CR's offense band; raise
toward the cap only while that round reads under band") cannot be implemented where the defect
lives. The `priced at the floor … the cap is the price` ladder is in `templates.mjs valueCheck`
and is **per row**: it knows a row's own EV against a floor, and nothing about the round. The
round lives in `generate.mjs priceTheRound`.

So A1 needs either round state threaded into the value ladder, or a late pass that brings a
payoff down once the round is known — the second fits the engine's existing "a normalisation
runs after everything that can change its input" pattern, but `priceTheRound` currently re-solves
attack rows only, not save rows.

**And the target is not the band.** Measured against our own offense gate, print trips its soft
tier on 27% of CR 11-15 blocks and 23% of CR 16-20, up to +7 over, with **zero** of 329 blocks
failing it. Clamping to band would push high-CR sheets below print. The forty's two flagged
blocks are both inside print's range (Brass Tyrant +5 where print's median at that band is +5;
the bell swarm +4 under print's +6). The real complaint is narrower: the ladder sets a payoff at
the cap *regardless of what the sheet needs*, which is why the Brass Tyrant's strike is 71
against its own fist's 34. The round can be in range while the single row is lopsided.

4b asks to move armour, headers, the immunity split, the Multiattack text and section order into

# The chassis row's lost provenance — the investigation, and the fix

**Found in one place:** `generate.mjs` ~2425, the rename rebuild. Every chassis row was
reconstructed as a fresh object from an **explicit allow-list**, so any field not named there
was dropped silently. Measured against every field a donor row can actually carry:

| field | rows | rules branch | rename branch |
|---|---|---|---|
| `imposes` | 53 | dropped | dropped |
| `gated_on` | 23 | dropped | dropped |
| `engulf_engine` | 11 | dropped | dropped |
| `engine` | 3 | dropped | dropped |
| `rules` | 117 | kept | **dropped** |
| `gate` | 43 | **dropped** | kept |

**It had already cost three fixes.** The branch's own comment recorded the first: *"the Swallow's
keys were stamped and lost here — sprint run 2026-10-12"*, patched then by adding one field to
the list. The second was A2's `imposes`, which is why tagging 8 seed cards and 53 donor rows
moved coverage not at all. The third was the Multiattack's provenance: that row takes the rules
branch, which strips everything, so there was no flag to narrow the noun fix with — and that is
why the noun fix needed a workaround in the first place.

**The fix:** spread the source row and override what changes. An allow-list has to be remembered;
a spread cannot forget. Only `wildcard_slot` is removed, because it is re-expressed as `wildcard`.

**Blast radius, measured before landing.** Four field types newly reach the sheet, on ~90 rows.
Checking every reader: `gated_on` and `engine` are read **only** off `scaled.sections`, the donor
copy that always had them, so carrying them forward is inert. `engulf_engine` has exactly one
reader on the rebuilt sections, and that site already text-matched for the same rows via the
Grappled-escape-DC phrase. No donor row on the rules branch carries a `gate`, so that column of
the table is moot in practice.

### One pin's route changed, its outcome kept

### The 4c defect the regression found

The Ancient Gold Dragon came out with no CR, no PB, no stats and no rows. Cause: the 2014 exact
path served it, and `exact2014Printing` built a fresh object that **renamed
`type_and_alignment` to `size_type_alignment` and omitted `attributes` and `format`**. Every
downstream reader expects the canonical legacy names, so the checker read a served book block as
an engine-shaped one, found nothing, and failed it for "WIS undefined + PB 2" and "no attack row
at all". The 5.1 corpus already carries those names; passing them through was the whole fix.

**The gate never saw this.** My `exact-2014.spec.js` asserted the fields I had invented, so it
passed while the block was unusable. Nine tests, all green, all testing my own naming.

### And a deeper one, still open

With the names fixed the checker reads the served blocks — and **the real published 2014 text
fails our own 2014 lint**, 12 findings on the Owlbear, 27 on the Ancient Gold Dragon, 28 on the
Vampire: `phrasing-to-hit`, `term-caps`, `damage-type-caps`, `phrasing-hit-marker`.

Our 2014 lint is calibrated to the ENGINE's 2014 idiom, not to the book. Checking the book
against our own style rules is backwards — "print overrules reviewers" is the standing ruling,
and a block served verbatim from print is the strongest possible case for it.

**The fix shape:** a block whose `generation` says it was served verbatim (`source: 'srd-5.1'`,
`calls: 0`) is exempt from the phrasing lint, as print's own rows are exempt elsewhere. Not
started — it needs its own landing, and the regression set re-run after it.
