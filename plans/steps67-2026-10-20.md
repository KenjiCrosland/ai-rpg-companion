## Step 7 — the homebrew pipeline

The brief's retirement reads: *"the concept's VERB outranks the noun" as a route decision →
dispatch on class and print_body; style keys move into the resolve call on the homebrew path.*

**What the code already had.** The route call ranks up to three lines, quotes the GM's words
for each, and the engine verifies every quote (`quotedPick`). Since 2026-10-16 it also answers
`cue_verdicts` — whether each key word the engine found really means that chassis's mechanic
here — so the override was already gated on the call's own judgment. The keys were still the
**decision**: a confirmed key promoted its line over the call's own top pick.

**What landed (2026-10-20).** On the homebrew path, when the call's chosen pick carried a
verified quote, the override stands down and says so in the trace. The keys remain as (a) the
menu the call is shown and (b) the fallback when no pick verifies or the family fell to its
plain line. Every other path is untouched, and the blank roll is untouched because the
retirement rests on the call quoting *the GM's own words* and a blank roll has none.

The route prompt now also demands one consistent answer: **a cue you confirm is a pick you
make.** Confirming a key and then ranking a different chassis is one answer contradicting
itself. This is the mechanism that is supposed to preserve the override's good outcomes
without the engine overruling the call.

`chooseLine` takes `path`; the component now makes the *choice* after the path call resolves
(the two calls still race, so there is no added latency) and the *parse* in `routeOn`.

**Why this needed a probe, not just pins.** This flip was attempted and reverted on 2026-10-16
because it broke six pins. Those pins mock the model's answer, so they cannot show whether the
call now ranks correctly — they only show what the engine does with a fixed answer. Three of
them (the giant crab → grappler, the miller who turns into a boar → shifter, the drowned wife →
phantom) are cases where the mocked pick is the *wrong* line and the key corrects it. They pass
unchanged because they pass no path, but that means the suite no longer covers live homebrew.
`scripts/probe-line.mjs` closes that gap: one route call per sample, the same answer fed to
both mechanisms, so the comparison carries no call variance. The three pin cases ride as
labelled traps.

## Step 6 — the retirements, inventoried against the code

Steps 2–5 already retired most of the table. What is left is mostly **legitimate fallback**,
which the brief allows ("the matcher stands in only when the call failed"). The guard test the
brief asks for must therefore test *where* a rule decides, not whether it exists.

home.

## The homebrew path's dropped Multiattack — found, and it was the chassis

The Squallwing Drake shipped one Claw and no Multiattack at CR 7. My first read of the trace
blamed the GM's named parts replacing the chassis attack rows and the Multiattack leaving with
them. **That was wrong.** `landAuthored` rewrites a Multiattack it finds (`if (ma && …)`) and
never creates one, and the real cause is upstream: **the `dragon-2` chassis is a CR 2 donor
with no Multiattack at all**, scaled to CR 7. `scaleDonor` scales numbers, not shape, so a
low-CR donor carries its low-CR row set up the ladder.

Ten of the ninety donors carry no Multiattack, and eight of those are CR 1–3 bodies that are
perfectly correct at their own CR. Print settles what happens when they are scaled: a
Multiattack on **110 of 113 blocks at CR 5 and up**, against 91 of 106 single-attack blocks
below CR 1. Modal count: **two at CR 5–10, three at CR 11+** (measured, 108 blocks). One
attack row cited N times is print's own idiom — 70 of its 110 CR 5+ Multiattacks name a single
row ("the dragon makes three Rend attacks") — so a one-row chassis gets exactly that.

It lands in `scaleDonor`, **beside the swarm strip and before the per-hit solve**, so the round
is priced for the attack count the sheet actually makes. Putting it later would double the
round past its card, which is the mirror of the bug the swarm strip's comment records.

The swarm exemption is **structural** (`donor.archetypes` includes `swarm`), not a dependence
on the caller passing `oneAttack`: print's seven swarms each make one attack and the checker
hard-fails a swarm that carries a Multiattack, so a swarm chassis must never gain one even if a
caller forgets the flag. `swarm-9` and `swarm-17` do carry an inherited one in the library,
which `oneAttack` still strips — that is pre-existing and unchanged.
