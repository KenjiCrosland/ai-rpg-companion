## What landed, and what the trace says it does

The route Grask's defect took, from the GM forty:

```
signature idea (trait): "When Grask hits a creature with his Ironjaw attack, he can
                        attempt to grapple the target as part of the same attack…"
                        [trait/always/condition] [family: payoff]
composed candidates: none trait — the payoff family composes a rider
                        and this is the trait seat
seat authored the row: trait — the call refused every template for no_fit
audit rename: "Jaw-Lock" -> "Locking Grip"
```

**The trigger was never available to route on.** The call stamped it `always`, not `on_hit`, even
though the idea's own text opens "When Grask hits a creature". Anything keyed on the trigger would
have missed this.

**The family knew.** `GRAMMARS.payoff.section === 'rider'` — the payoff family composes a rider on
the creature's own attack, by design, and `composedReason` says exactly that before declining.
`normaliseTag` already encodes "what an idea IS beats what the seat asked for" (an action idea
triggered `on_hit` becomes a rider), and could not reach this case: the trait seat **forces**
`section: 'trait'`, and `trait/always` is legal, so the tag returns before the family is consulted.

The correction sits beside `normaliseTag`, reading declared fields only — family, that family's own
grammar section, and effect:

```js
const ridesTheAttack = effect === 'condition'
  && section !== 'rider'
  && GRAMMARS[family]?.section === 'rider';
```

Scoped to `condition` because that is the ruling. Measured over three saved forties: **8 sheets**
carry trait + payoff + condition. A further **~10** carry a `damage` payoff at a trait seat — the
same architectural mismatch, since payoff composes a rider for damage too — and are deliberately
left alone pending the owner.

Unit-level: 6 tests, sensitivity-checked (disable the predicate and the spec fails). The re-seat
fires on Grask's shape, and does **not** fire on a damage payoff, on a `zone` condition (its grammar
composes a trait), on an idea already seated as a rider, or on a family with no grammar.

**Then three findings, none of which a unit test could have produced.**

### 1. The authoring path ignores the idea's section

```
seat authored the row: trait — the call refused every template for no_fit
    ("a passive stance keyed to an ally within reach that grants Grask crit ")
    and wrote a trait instead: "Shield-Wall Anchor" -> Traits
```

The idea was `rider/on_hit`. The fit call refused the composed rider options. The seat then authored
a **trait** regardless. So the ruling is not yet true: re-seating gets an idea onto the rider path,
and a refusal still lands it as a trait. Fix 2 needs a second site — authoring either honours the
section or refuses the row.

### 2. `v-small` #4 cannot replay the known-bad case

The shipped block has **no grapple anywhere** — zero occurrences of grapple, grip or clamp in the
whole roll. The input is `name: Grask Ironjaw | orc war chief`, and the trace reads:

```
[concept] mechanics: none claimed
[concept] signature elements: iron-jawed orc war chief [body] |
          rallying bellow that drives the warband [behavior] |
          iron-shod fists and jagged blade in the front line [object]
```

**The grapple on typed40 #1 was the idea call's own invention that roll, not something the GM
asked for.** This input therefore cannot be relied on to produce the defect, and the done-when
("the grapple sits on the Longsword row with an escape DC") is **unproven, not met and not failed**.

The canary needs an input that actually asks for a hold — "orc war chief who grabs you by the
throat" or similar. I have not edited `v-small.json`; it is your batch and the entry's `green` field
would need rewriting with it.

### 3. The risk, which is the owner's to rule on

The re-seat trusts the call's `family` and `effect`. This roll shows the call wrong about both:

> "Grask fights from the center of his shield-wall: while at least one ally that can hear him is
> within reach of him, his Iron Jaw attack scores a Critical Hit on a roll of 19 or 20, and he
> cannot be Frightened." **`[rider/on_hit/condition]` `[family: payoff]`**

A passive stance granting a crit range and fear immunity is not a condition rider. The call stamped
it `payoff` + `condition`, and my correction moved it to the rider path.

**No harm landed, only because finding 1 is still open** — authoring ignored the section and wrote
the trait it should have been. Close finding 1 without addressing this and the same idea becomes a
wrong rider welded onto the Longsword.

So the two findings interact, and finishing fix 2 in the obvious order makes the sheet worse before
it makes it better. That is why this is a report rather than another push.
