# Brief — step 9: species traits, class features, feats; and the re-roll path — 2026-10-13

Owner (2026-10-13): "If someone names 'lizardfolk' they need to have the Hold Breath trait verbatim. If they are a lizardfolk monk they need those class features too. We're missing an opportunity to take feats like Great Weapon Master or Brawler." And: users say per-entry re-rolls "seem incredibly random, as though it doesn't take the whole creature into consideration."

## 1. What the re-roll path is today (the complaint is accurate)

`rerollEntry` / `rerollSection` (`format-2024/generate.mjs`) is the ONE remaining free-text rules path. It predates the template catalog (2026-09-21: "rules text has one source, the catalog") and was never moved onto it. Its prompt hands the model: the monster's name, CR and combat role; the OTHER entries' **names only** (no texts); the current entry; the number skeletons. It does not hand over the creature's concept or the user's words (`generation.userConcept`, the clause record), the type, size or lore, the other rows' texts, the print canon, or the engine's own catalog. The model is told to invent "a brand-new ability filling the same role" — so it does, from the name and the CR alone. Lint then prices the number skeletons. That is why a re-roll on a lizardfolk brute can come back as a psychic scream: nothing in the prompt says lizardfolk.

**Fix (step 9b):** route the per-entry re-roll through the reskin's own path — the idea call conditioned on the WHOLE creature (user's words, clauses, type/size, the sheet's rows, the print canon), then the fit against the catalog legal for that section and CR, the engine rendering the frame and pricing it; the library seed stands in on a miss; a free-text answer is never shipped. The user's steering text on a section re-roll is a clause. The whole-sheet re-roll (`rerollSection`) takes the same route per slot. The prompt text that remains (the idea call) carries the creature's concept and every other row's text, so the new entry reads as THIS creature's. Measurement: re-roll each row of six saved sheets (print, parts, one-liner, Name field, both editions) three times; a re-roll is a pass when it is a catalog form, priced, and binds to the creature's words or to a row it cites; zero free-text rows.

## 2. Species traits, class features, feats (step 9a) — closed tables, no call, in print's idiom

**Species.** The SRD 5.2 corpus has no Lizardfolk (it is not an SRD creature), so species traits are a hand-authored table keyed by the species word, each trait written in print's own idiom from the corpus (the Crocodile's "can hold its breath for 1 hour" → "The lizardfolk can hold its breath for 15 minutes."; Pack Tactics, Sunlight Sensitivity, Amphibious, Keen Smell, Darkvision in the Senses line, a swim or climb speed in the Speed line). First table: lizardfolk, kobold, goblin, hobgoblin, bugbear, orc, gnoll, dwarf, elf, halfling, gnome, dragonborn (breath as the body's nova), tiefling, aasimar, goliath, tabaxi, kenku, tortle, aarakocra, triton, firbolg, minotaur, centaur, satyr, merfolk, sahuagin, yuan-ti, troglodyte, grung, harengon, owlin. A species word in the words lands its traits verbatim, header lines included, with the clause as provenance. A print alias (kobold → Kobold Warrior) keeps print's body as today; the species table fills only what print does not already carry.

**Class features.** Keyed by the class word, in the idiom of the SRD's NPC statblocks (the Assassin's Sneak Attack and Evasion, the Berserker's Bloodied Frenzy, the Gladiator's Parry, the Knight's Leadership, the Priest's Divine Aid, the Mage's Spellcasting, the Scout's senses, the Warrior Veteran's Multiattack). One table per class: barbarian (Rage as a Bloodied-keyed damage trait, Reckless Attack), fighter (Second Wind, Action Surge, a style word → the existing style rules), monk (Martial Arts unarmed row at the sheet's dice, Flurry of Blows bonus action, Unarmored Defense on the AC line, Stunning Strike as a save rider, Deflect Attacks as a reaction), paladin (Divine Smite rider, Lay on Hands, an aura trait), ranger (Favored Enemy/Hunter's Mark as a rider, two attacks), rogue (Sneak Attack — already in the feature table —, Cunning Action, Evasion, Uncanny Dodge), the casters (bard, cleric, druid, sorcerer, warlock, wizard → Spellcasting with a class-flavored list from the existing menu, plus one signature: Bardic Inspiration, Channel Divinity, Wild Shape as a Shape-Shift row, Eldritch Blast as a ranged row, Arcane Recovery). Every damage-bearing feature prices through the shared budget the way the feature table's Trip, Riposte and GWM already do (the final price pass solves the round); traits with no damage are free; Unarmored Defense and Tough move header numbers through the declared-fields path so the checker reads them as user-set.

**Feats.** The feature table grows from its fifteen to the feats a GM names: Great Weapon Master (there), Polearm Master (a reaction attack when a creature enters reach; a bonus-action butt-end strike), Sentinel (a reaction attack that sets Speed to 0), Sharpshooter (no disadvantage at long range; cover ignored), Shield Master (a shove rider), Charger (the charge rider the brute-charger already carries), Mobile/Speedy (a speed line and no opportunity attacks after a melee attack), Grappler (an Advantage rider against a grappled target), Dual Wielder (a bonus-action second weapon attack), Crossbow Expert, Savage Attacker, Tough (HP), Alert (Initiative), Mage Slayer, War Caster, Brawler → the 2024 Unarmed Fighting idiom (an Unarmed Strike row at 1d6/1d8 + STR and a grapple damage rider) alongside Tavern Brawler. Wording is ours (the engine's forms); the shapes are print's. A feat named in the words is a clause and lands with provenance.

**Order.** 9a-i species table and landing (one landing, the twelve + a species six); 9a-ii class tables (one landing per two or three classes, the twelve + a class six); 9a-iii feats (one landing); 9b the re-roll route. Canaries every landing; the twelve every landing; a step-9 feature batch (~24: species × class × feat × edition) at close-out, frozen like `features-2026-10.json`. The forty is unchanged; it runs on the critic's green light at close-out.

**Not in this step:** multiclass, subclass features, spell lists beyond the existing menu, and the character sheet proper.

## 3. The kit layer — how species, classes and feats compose without a mess (owner's questions, 2026-10-13)

**One door.** Species, class and feat are one kind of thing: a word-keyed KIT of header lines and rows, where every row is either an SRD-verbatim row lifted from the corpus and renamed to the creature, or a catalog shape with fixed fills. No free text. `features.mjs` grows into the kits module with three data tables; one landing function in the slot the feature table lands in today (after parts, before the signature path); the checker reads a `kit` provenance stamp the way it reads `feature`.

**One set of rules.** Print wins (a species that is a print alias keeps print's body; the kit fills only rows print lacks, by name). One nova (a kit's nova yields to the chassis's). One budget (damage-bearing rows price through the final price pass; traits are free; header numbers go through declared fields). One cap at print's density by CR, with a fixed ladder for what yields: (1) the GM's own nouns and numbers never; (2) free rows the words owe — a species' Hold Breath and header lines, a class's identity feature; (3) features keyed on the GM's words — Trip, Riposte, GWM, a named feat; (4) implied extras — a species flavor trait, a class's secondary features, a second feat. One dedupe by name. One provenance stamp (kit + clause, or the implied key).

**Kits bind before they add.** A monk's Martial Arts does not add an Unarmed Strike beside claws and a bite; the claws and bite ARE the monk's weapons (Flurry reads "makes two Razor Sharp Claws or Vicious Bite attacks as a Bonus Action"; Stunning Strike rides those rows; Unarmored Defense moves the AC line). Contradictions resolve toward what the GM typed (a monk with a greatsword keeps the greatsword and GWM; Martial Arts does not apply to it).

**The implied tier.** Kits plug into the catalog's key matcher (stems, sense-denials). (a) Class-adjacent trades are PRINT bodies first: ninja → Assassin, berserker → Berserker, holy knight/paladin → Knight, priest/cleric → Priest, wizard → Mage, ranger/tracker → Scout, spy → Spy, thug → Tough, veteran/fighter → Warrior Veteran, pirate, druid, cultist, gladiator, noble. (b) Synonym keys on the class kits (monk: martial artist, pugilist, unarmed, fists, ki; rogue: thief, cutpurse, burglar; barbarian: berserker, savage; paladin: crusader, templar, oathsworn; ranger: hunter, woodsman; warlock: pact, patron, hexblade; bard: minstrel, skald) with denials (monkey, monkfish; "shadow" on an Undead is the Shadow). (c) Flavor variants keyed on modifier words — the SRD ships one subclass per class (Berserker, College of Lore, Life Domain, Circle of the Land, Champion, Warrior of the Open Hand, Oath of Devotion, Hunter, Thief, Draconic Sorcery, Fiend Patron, Evoker), so Open Hand is verbatim and a shadow monk is AUTHORED from SRD pieces (the Shadow's Shadow Stealth, a Shadow Step as the teleport frame bound to dim light, Darkvision). (d) Consistency with the routed style (skulker + no weapon → monk-shadow; skulker + blades → rogue; charger + heavy weapon → GWM). (e) Feats implied by verbs (brawler, sharpshooter, "keeps everyone at bay" → Polearm Master/Sentinel). Guardrails: implied kits are the bottom rung and yield first; every implied row's trace says "implied" and names the key; one implied class per sheet; an explicit class or species word suppresses implication; every row is a catalog form or SRD text, priced, deletable per entry.

**Eligibility is MIND, not type** (owner: "we shouldn't restrict to humanoids — fiends and fey can learn these skills"). A class kit needs a sapient mind (the identity pass already classifies it; print: the Pit Fiend, Night Hag and Rakshasa cast, the Vampire is a swordsman, dragons have spell lists). Beasts, oozes, plants and mindless constructs get none unless the GM's words grant the mind ("an awakened bear who trained as a monk"); "a mindless hulk" on a Humanoid gets none. Body requirements are PER ROW: weapon features need a wielder or a named weapon; unarmed features need natural weapons or hands (a fiend's claws are its monk weapons); casting features need only the mind; movement features need the body to move that way. A row the body cannot meet yields with a trace line; the kit does not fail. The implied tier obeys the same gate (a ninja can be implied, a wolf cannot).

**Trigger ≠ eligibility** (owner, 2026-10-13: "we can't always default to class features if they are eligible; sometimes a GM just wants a monster with monster traits"). Mind decides whether a class kit MAY land; the GM's words decide whether it DOES. The default is monster traits. A class kit lands only on a positive cue: explicit (a class word, a feat name) or implied by a TRAINED-SKILL trade or verb (ninja, assassin, duelist, swordsman, sharpshooter, brawler, "trained as", "studied under", "fights like a") — a list far narrower than ROLE_NOUN (hunter, warden, toll-collector, gardener, butcher are jobs, not training, and land nothing). Never a trigger: a sapient mind alone, the type, a print head ("a pit fiend"), a creature-only prompt ("an owlbear"), a species word alone ("lizardfolk" owes Hold Breath, not a class), the routed style alone. Style consistency only SHAPES a kit a cue already triggered ("moves in the shadows" picks the shadow variant of the monk that "ninja" summoned; by itself it is a skulker with monster traits, as today). The trace names the word that opened the door. Measurement: the step-9 batch carries a "no class" control group — print heads, one-liners with jobs but no training, species-only prompts — that must land zero class rows.
